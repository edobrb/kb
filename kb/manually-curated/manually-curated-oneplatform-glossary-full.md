# OnePlatform Glossary

## Definition  <!-- REQUIRED · source-silence: cite-only -->

This page is the **disambiguation index** for **TeamSystem OnePlatform** — a lean confusables register, deliberately **not** an encyclopedia. Its job is narrow: given a bare or ambiguous term, answer **"which X do you mean, and what is its canonical name?"**, and flag what that term must **never be merged** into.

**Descriptions live elsewhere, by reference.** Per-term definitions belong to the typed wiki pages, the [[TeamSystem Product Catalog]], and the [[OnePlatform Architecture and CityMap]]; this page **points** to them (wikilinks) and never duplicates a description. The load-bearing concepts are curated with one dominant sense each in the [[OnePlatform Essential Glossary]] — the first stop for resolving a bare confusable to its default meaning; this page holds the full set of niche and named senses.

**How to read this page (keyed by what you type).** The primary lookup is the `## Resolution Index` below: **key by the surface form a user or agent actually TYPES** — a product name or an abbreviation — and it returns the canonical name (+ a pointer to the page that describes it) or, for a dangerous collision, an `⚠ ambiguous` warning that must NOT be auto-expanded. Every commercial product on the [[TeamSystem Product Catalog]] resolves there (Catalog ⊆ Glossary — enforced by `tools/lint-catalog-glossary-xref.py`). The deeper never-merge structure — generation pairs, same-name-across-domains collisions, single-concept facets, the non-product structural confusables (Agents, IAM, the CityMap `L`-vs-`L1..8` flag) — lives in `## Confusables`; the platform's load-bearing canonical anchors in `## Canonical Register`.

It is **derived**: a hand-maintained register projected for retrieval, for the `/api/glossary` endpoint (which parses the `## Confusables` tables below), and for `tools/glossary-derive.py`, which emits the per-consumer slices (lookup, confusable-card, never-translate, canonical-map, per-domain) the ingestion and synthesis steps consume.[^1] It is not itself a citable `source:`.

The governing rule for every entry: **use the canonical name, and never merge a legacy system into its successor.** The load-bearing never-merge cues:

- **ICE** (Integrated Centralized Experience) — not **OneFront** (legacy frontend platform); **Polar** (ICE design system) — not **Vapor** (legacy).
- **Hermes 2** (Kafka streaming) — not **Hermes 1** (legacy Azure Service Bus), and neither is **Integration Hub** (a separate point-to-point bus, not a Hermes version).
- **Metering M3** (current) — not **Metering M1** (legacy), and neither is the **MongoDB Atlas M10/M30/M40** tier family (unrelated `M` collision).
- **The "?IC" abbreviations** — `FIC` = Fatture in Cloud, `DIC` = Dipendenti in Cloud, `COMIC` = Commercialisti in Cloud are ruled; bare **CIC** and **AIC** have multiple claimants and must **NEVER be auto-expanded**.
- **DWH** (Data Warehouse, L7 Data) — not **Warehouse / WMS** (physical SCM warehousing). Same root, opposite domains.

## Two Registers — the governing design (KB 0.5.0)  <!-- NEW · the frozen canonicalization baseline -->

This glossary is **two-register**. Every entry belongs to exactly one register, except the dual-sense terms pinned in `## Confusables → Cluster — Two-register seam`:

1. **Commercial / legal register — CONTRACT-AUTHORITATIVE.** For every term defined in the Contratto Unico OnePlatform Definizioni blocks (Condizioni Generali + Condizioni Integrative), **the contract definition is canonical**.[^13][^14] These entries live in `## Commercial / Legal Register` below, translated IT→EN 1:1 with the Italian defined term preserved in parentheses wherever the English rendering risks losing legal precision, and with per-term contract-clause provenance. For legally-exact wording an answer quotes the `raw/` Italian original (hard rule 8).
2. **Architectural register — REPO/ADR-AUTHORITATIVE.** The platform's technical vocabulary (Hermes, ICE, TS ID, Policy Manager, Workspace, Metering, Module Federation, Item Registry, …) stays sourced from the pinned repos, ADRs, and dev-portal docs. **The contract is SILENT on every architectural term** — no legal framing is forced onto these entries. They live in `## Canonical Register` (the architectural register) and `## Confusables`, unchanged in authority.

**The precedence rule at the seam (intent-keyed).** Some surfaces carry BOTH senses — most acutely *Prodotto/Product*, *Servizio/Service*, *Piattaforma/Platform*. For those, resolution keys on **query intent**, never on a blanket authority:

- A **commercial / contractual / legal** question — pricing, Fees (Corrispettivi), activation, Order (Ordine), licensing, liability, termination, withdrawal, data-processing roles, who may use what — resolves to the **contract sense** (commercial register).
- An **architectural / integration / runtime** question — APIs, services, components, deployment, events, authN/authZ, composition — resolves to the **repo/ADR sense** (architectural register).
- A genuinely ambiguous question **clarifies** instead of guessing.

This is continuous with the existing source-precedence model ([`docs/conventions/source-precedence.md`](../../docs/conventions/source-precedence.md)): that model already splits **technical facts** (where `repo-code@SHA` outranks every documentation surface) from **product/commercial intent** (where the documentation order decides and code does not participate). The Contratto Unico (`authority: normative`) slots into that model as the **top authority for the commercial/legal register** — a continuation of the same boundary, not a new mechanism. A same-fact conflict between the contract and any other source on a *commercial/legal* fact resolves to the contract; the contract never participates in *technical-fact* conflicts.

**Draft caveat (blocking for legal citation, not for canonicalization).** Both contract files are **drafts** (internal markers: "Da rivedere una volta finalizzata la bozza di Contratto"; internal article cross-references are partially inconsistent — e.g. Comunicazione di Ritiro is pointed at §20.1 while Servizio Obsoleto/Sostitutivo at §21.1, both defined in the same Ritiro article). The docx→md conversion also dropped the article numbering (headings survive as bullets). Provenance below therefore cites **article NAME + Definizioni block position**; reconstructed article numbers are given only where the contract's own cross-references establish them. Verify against the `.docx` originals before quoting legally-exact wording.

## Pre-Scan (how ingestion agents use this register)  <!-- OPTIONAL · source-silence: cite-only (when present) -->

Ingestion agents do **not** load this page into context or call a tool per word. `tools/glossary-derive.py` builds a deterministic **surface set** from the Canonical Register (canonical names + aliases + never-translate tokens) plus the `## Never-Translate Patterns` rules; a pure-Python string match hands the LLM only the tiny per-source slice of entries actually present, so the model disambiguates with the right canonical names and flags any new candidate term as a `glossary_delta` for maintainer review.

**Alias hygiene (register rule).** Surface matching is **case-insensitive**, so casing variants of one token (`FiC`, `Fic`) are the SAME surface — the register lists it once, in canonical form. **An alias is only a genuine other-name** — a former name (`Company Registry` → Item Registry, `Prosystem` → Giada, `Personal Area` → My Value Center) or a real cross-domain collision. It is **never** an abbreviation listed as a synonym of its own expansion, a team name, or a same-string language variant. **Acronyms, brand and product names are language-neutral** (`CAF` is CAF in any language) and carry no `(en)`/`(it)` tag; language tags mark only genuine translation pairs (`Paghe(it)` vs `Payroll(en)`).

## Confusables

Close-named clusters, generation pairs, same-name-across-domains collisions, acronym overloads, and speech-to-text homophones. Each `### family` table answers: what does the **Term** mean, and what is it **confusable** with — so an agent never conflates a legacy system with its successor, two same-named things across domains, or a real service with an ASR artifact. The `Confusable?` column names the trap and the canonical disambiguation (the load-bearing never-merge cue parsed by `/api/glossary`). Full descriptions live at the linked wiki pages / [[TeamSystem Product Catalog]] / [[OnePlatform Architecture and CityMap]] — this section carries only the disambiguation.

### Cluster — Two-register seam — commercial (contract) sense vs architectural (repo/ADR) sense  <!-- NEW · KB 0.5.0 -->

Continuous with the technical-fact vs commercial-intent split in `docs/conventions/source-precedence.md`.

## Authority Pointers

Where each authored `[[Page]]` pointer in this register resolves in the **KB 0.6.x source corpus**.
The rows above point at documents by the name a human wrote; a corpus addresses documents by
`source_id`, and this table is the declared mapping between the two. It is read by
`engine_v2.graph.load_markdown_registry` as a declaration table, **not** as a relation of concepts —
a row here mints no concept.

Three states, and the third is the point: a `source_id` **binds**; `—` means the corpus has **no
such document** and the pointer emits no reference at all; a pointer **absent from this table**
passes through unchanged and reports as unresolved. So "we looked and it is not here" never reads
the same as "nobody looked".

**Binding rule.** A pointer binds only where the evidence is in the source's own identity — its
`source_id` path names the same thing — or where the intake tree declares the document. A pointer
that would need a judgement about which of several documents "is" the authority is left `—` with the
reason stated, because inventing an authority assignment is worse than declaring the gap.

## Common Misconceptions  <!-- OPTIONAL · source-silence: cite-only (when present) -->

- "ICE and OneFront are the same platform." -> ICE is the current microfrontend platform; OneFront (1F) is the legacy platform it supersedes.
- "Polar is a newer version of Vapor." -> Polar is a distinct current design system that supersedes Vapor; "Vapor 3" is still the legacy system, not Polar.
- "Hermes 1 is Integration Hub." -> Three distinct things: Hermes 1 (legacy Azure Service Bus streaming), Hermes 2 (current Kafka streaming), and Integration Hub (a separate on-prem<->cloud bus via the Girino proxy).
- "Metering M1/M3 are MongoDB Atlas M-tiers." -> The Metering M1/M3 generations share only the 'M' prefix with the Atlas M10/M30/M40 cluster sizes; they are unrelated and must never be merged.
- "M2M, the M2M Token, and Client-Credentials Flow are one thing." -> M2M is the machine identity type; the M2M Token is the legacy manually-issued JWT it is moving off; Client-Credentials Flow is the standard OAuth grant that replaces the legacy token.
- "Powerboard is a mistranslation of dashboard." -> Powerboard is a real ICE Experience service that builds product dashboards.
- "CIC means Cassa in Cloud." -> Bare CIC has at least three claimants (Cassa / Contabilità / Commercialisti in Cloud) and is NEVER auto-expanded; only context disambiguates. Same guard for AIC (the dropped former name of TS Waste).
- "Coffee in Cloud is a TeamSystem product." -> No — Coffee In Cloud (sometimes abbreviated CIC or CC in that material) is a FICTIONAL product used only as a worked example in ICE developer documentation. It is never a real SKU; do not add it to the catalog or expand CIC/CC to it. The real CC is **CC Suite = Change Capital**.
- "Tigran is a legacy product." -> Tigran (ModeFinance) is the CURRENT credit- and financial-risk platform; the catalog's "TIGRAN Legacy" is a legacy variant, not the product's lifecycle.
- "DWH is the Warehouse capability." -> DWH = Data Warehouse (analytics, L7 Data); Warehouse/WMS is physical SCM warehousing. Same root word, opposite domains.
- "Personal Area and My Value Center are two different things." -> Personal Area is the FORMER NAME of My Value Center (alias); only "My Personal Area" (the monetization data-product consumer) is genuinely distinct.
- "GPS in TSE Industry 4.0 is satellite GPS." -> It is the Global Planning System, a materials/production-planning module.
- "TeamSystem Fashion / Wine / Oil are Enterprise modules." -> They are distinct vertical ERP products, not modules of TeamSystem Enterprise.
- "The contract defines what a Core Service is." -> No. The Contratto Unico defines ~136 commercial/legal terms and is SILENT on every architectural term (Hermes, ICE, TS ID, Core Services, M1/M3). Contract authority applies per register, not per corpus.
- "Servizio in the contract = a platform service." -> No — three senses: the contract Servizio (customer-facing deliverable through the Piattaforma), a Core Service/microservice (architecture), and M1's 'servizio' (= Feature, the monetization unit). Route on intent.
- "TeamSystem HR is one thing." -> In the Condizioni Integrative "TeamSystem HR" IS the mobile app of the Servizio TeamSystem HR; in the catalog it is the mid-market HR platform. Route on register.
- "TS Pay is just a product." -> In the contract TS Pay is the COMPANY TeamSystem Payments S.r.l. (a Bank-of-Italy-registered payment institution); the Payment Services run under the separate Contratto Payments on the separate Piattaforma TS Pay. The catalog "TS Pay" is the product surface.
- "Terzo Beneficiario has one definition." -> Two: the CG (broad — contractual relations; art. 2359 co.1 nn.1-2) and the CI (narrow — a client of the Customer; art. 2359 co.1 n.1). The CI prevails where it applies.
- "SFA is Sales Force Automation." -> Only in a CRM context; in the manufacturing / TSE Industry-4.0 context SFA = Shop Floor Analytics. Never auto-expand.
